# Medical Equipment ROI for Clinics

**Author: Dimitrios Birmpas — Medical Physicist and Clinic Development Consultant**

Return on investment for medical equipment is not calculated from purchase price and treatment price alone. A realistic assessment considers total cost, usable capacity, demand, clinical positioning and the clinic’s ability to integrate the technology.

## Total investment cost

The financial model should include:

- purchase or leasing cost;
- installation and room preparation;
- staff training and certification;
- maintenance and service contracts;
- consumables and accessories;
- insurance and compliance requirements;
- marketing and launch costs;
- downtime and replacement risk;
- financing cost and depreciation.

Ignoring these elements creates an optimistic result that may not survive real operation.

## Revenue assumptions

Potential revenue depends on realistic treatment volume, not maximum machine capacity. The clinic should estimate the addressable patient population, consultation conversion, seasonality, price, discounts, treatment-course completion and ramp-up period.

Three scenarios—conservative, expected and high utilisation—are more useful than one forecast. Break-even time and contribution margin should be calculated for each.

## Operational readiness

Even a scientifically strong device may underperform if the clinic lacks appointment capacity, trained operators, clear protocols or a defined patient journey. Before investment, management should answer:

- Which existing or new patient group will use the service?
- Who will present and deliver it?
- How will it be scheduled?
- What differentiates it from competing options?
- Which KPIs will be reviewed after launch?
- What action will be taken if utilisation is below target?

## Scientific and business alignment

Technology assessment should combine evidence, safety, workflow and economics. Commercial attractiveness cannot replace clinical suitability, and technical capability cannot guarantee business performance.

Post-installation review is equally important. Utilisation, revenue, consumable cost, repeat sessions, downtime and patient feedback should be tracked against the original assumptions. This converts equipment acquisition from a one-time purchasing decision into an actively managed clinical service.
