# Standard Operating Procedures for Modern Clinics

**Author: Dimitrios Birmpas — DB Clinic Development**

A Standard Operating Procedure converts experience into a repeatable method. In a clinic, SOPs help the team deliver consistent service even when workload increases, staff change or unusual situations occur.

## Where clinics need SOPs

Priority procedures commonly include:

- answering calls and recording enquiries;
- booking, confirming and rescheduling appointments;
- patient reception and identity checks;
- consultation preparation and room turnover;
- informed consent and clinical documentation;
- photography and data handling;
- equipment start-up, checks and shutdown;
- stock management and expiry control;
- post-treatment instructions and follow-up;
- incident, complaint and emergency escalation.

An SOP should state the purpose, scope, responsible roles, required materials, sequence of actions, exceptions, records and review date.

## Procedures must reflect reality

Copying a generic manual rarely works. A procedure must match the clinic’s team, services, software, layout and risk profile. It should be detailed enough to guide action but short enough to be used during daily work.

Development should involve the people who perform the process. Their experience identifies practical obstacles and creates ownership. The procedure is then tested, corrected and approved.

## Training and monitoring

Publishing an SOP does not mean it has been implemented. Staff require explanation, demonstration and supervised practice. Managers also need a simple method to verify compliance, record deviations and update the process when conditions change.

SOPs should be version-controlled and reviewed regularly. A change in equipment, regulation, software, staffing or service design may require revision.

The real benefit is operational stability. Clear procedures reduce dependence on memory, minimise avoidable variation and make onboarding faster. They also allow clinic performance to be evaluated against an agreed standard rather than personal preferences.
